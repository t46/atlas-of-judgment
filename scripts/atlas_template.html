Plate III · The Rhetoric

Each standard has a voice

Not just which standard fires, but the shape of the inference itself. Six forms, assigned to every one of the 1,009,592 units by a locally-trained classifier (no API, no keywords-only shortcut): the norm invoked, the verification blocked, the rival explanation raised, the precedent cited, the reach questioned, the evidence weighed. The caption owns the method and its error.

Fig. 3 — The voice of each standard — six inference forms, all 1,009,592 units

Fig. 3 — Method: 600 units labeled by the analyst (an LLM reading each sentence), merged to six forms; a logistic classifier over local embeddings plus surface-marker features (5-fold CV accuracy 0.685, macro-F1 0.666; weakest class ANCHOR at F1 0.53 — read its cells gently) then labels the full corpus. A cheaper second annotator agreed with the gold labels only 42% of the time and was excluded — the construct is genuinely interpretive, and these are one consistent reading at scale, not ground truth. The signatures hold: reproducibility is 69% pure norm; statistical identifiability leads with blocked verification (43%) — without error bars, nothing can be known; novelty leans hardest on precedent. And weighing is where all the mercy lives: it is the only form in which praise occurs at scale (42% positive; every other form runs ≥88% negative).

Plate VI · The Minds

Five kinds of reviewer

Cluster 98,513 reviewers by which standards they habitually invoke, and five archetypes emerge. Each rose has twelve petals — one per standard — sized by how far that reviewer-kind leans on it relative to the field. The names are editorial; the petals are not.

Fig. 6 — k-means (k = 5, seed 7) over per-reviewer reasoning-standard profiles, official reviewers with ≥5 units, 2018–2026; a "reviewer" is one reviewer within one forum. The sparkline is the archetype's share of each year's reviewers. The nine-year story: the Gatekeeper — novelty and polish — is fading (25% → 15%), and the Stress-Tester — robustness, confounds, cost — is inheriting the field (19% → 26%). The Advocate runs 23 points less negative than every other kind.

Plate VIII · The Drift

Nine years of shifting scrutiny

The same taxonomy, applied to 1,009,592 units from every ICLR review 2018–2026 (the forum-level Direct track). Share of reviewer attention per year; the four biggest movers are named, the rest recede.

Fig. 8a — What reviewers inspect, 2018 → 2026
Fig. 8b — The standards they invoke, 2018 → 2026
Fig. 8c — The hardening: nine-year vitals

Fig. 8 — The hygiene turn: compute cost (+3.2pp), statistical rigor (+3.0pp), baselines and reproducibility rise; clarity (−4.9pp) and theory (−4.1pp) recede. Among standards, the novelty standard and presentation-as-trust each fall 3.5pp while cost-benefit and claim-evidence matching climb — scrutiny has drifted from what is this idea? toward what does the evidence cost, and does it cover the claim? Negativity holds steady near 77% all nine years. Assignment similarity is flat across years (0.81), so the drift is not an artifact of taxonomy transfer. And the field is hardening: the share of post-response judgments that soften has halved, 7.7% in 2018 to 3.4% in 2026 — rebuttals buy half as much as they used to.

Plate IX · The Rebuttal

Author responses barely move the needle

130,650 units sit after the author response (2018–2026). When the reviewer's judgment moves at all, it moves toward reinforcement two and a half times more often than concession — and no kind of response escapes that ratio.

Fig. 9a — Fate of a judgment after the author responds
Fig. 9b — What the response contained, and what happened

Fig. 9 — 9a: shares of all post-response units. 9b: among units whose judgment visibly moved, split by a keyword reading of the recorded trigger; red = weakened or reversed, brass = strengthened, grey = clarified only. Even fresh experiments strengthen the original judgment 2.9× more often than they soften it. Softening rates barely differ by topic (3.9–5.4% across all twelve objects).

Plate X · The Tribunal

Which criticism actually kills

13,704 ICLR 2026 papers with decisions. For each object of scrutiny: the acceptance rate of papers that drew at least one negative unit there, against those that didn't. Association, not causation — but the asymmetry is stark.

Fig. 10 — Acceptance rate with (●) vs without (○) negative units, per object

Fig. 10 — Novelty criticism carries the largest penalty (−10.6pp); statistical rigor and baseline complaints follow. At the other end, compute-cost and robustness criticism carry no penalty at all — reviewers complain about cost, but the tribunal does not convict on it. Decisions were never shown to the extraction pipeline; they are joined here for the first time.

Plate XI · The Gauntlet

Every new front of criticism costs survival

For each ICLR 2026 paper: on how many of the twelve objects did it draw at least one negative unit — and did it survive? Dot size is the number of papers standing at that breadth.

Fig. 11 — Acceptance rate by breadth of criticism

Fig. 11 — Survival falls almost linearly, roughly five points per additional front, from 57% at three fronts to 27% at nine or more. Few papers escape with fewer than four: the modal paper is criticized on seven of the twelve objects. Association, not causation — breadth of criticism and paper quality are entangled by design.

Plate XII · The Panel

Dissent, and the kinder judge

Where do reviewers of the same paper split — and how does the meta-reviewer differ from the panel they oversee?

Fig. 12 — Contested ground: share of forums where reviewers took opposite stances

Fig. 12 — A category counts as contested in a forum when at least two official reviewers touched it and landed on opposite valences. Reviewers split most on problem framing (38.6%) — whether the question matters — and least on robustness (16.3%), where the evidence speaks. Meta-reviewers, meanwhile, run 13.7pp less negative and 10.5pp more positive than the panels they summarize.
