Plate III · The Rhetoric
Corpus: ICLR 2018–2026 · forum-level track · 1,009,592 units

Each standard has a voice

Not just which standard fires, but the shape of the inference itself. Six forms, assigned to every one of the 1,009,592 units of the nine-year forum-level track (introduced fully in Plate IX; neighboring plates use the 410,586-unit 2026 track) by a locally-trained classifier (no API, no keywords-only shortcut): the norm invoked, the verification blocked, the rival explanation raised, the precedent cited, the reach questioned, the evidence weighed. The caption owns the method and its error.

Fig. 3 — The voice of each standard — six inference forms, all 1,009,592 units

Fig. 3 — How to read: each row is one reasoning standard; its bar splits that standard’s units by the inference form the classifier assigned, and the right column names the dominant form. Method: 600 units labeled by the analyst (an LLM reading each sentence), merged to six forms; a logistic classifier over local embeddings plus surface-marker features (5-fold CV accuracy 0.685, macro-F1 0.666; weakest class ANCHOR at F1 0.53 — read its cells gently) then labels the full corpus. A cheaper second annotator agreed with the gold labels only 42% of the time and was excluded — the construct is genuinely interpretive, and these are one consistent reading at scale, not ground truth. The signatures hold: reproducibility is 69% pure norm; statistical identifiability leads with blocked verification (43%) — without error bars, nothing can be known; novelty leans hardest on precedent. And weighing is where all the mercy lives: it is the only form in which praise occurs at scale (42% positive; every other form runs ≥88% negative).

Plate VI · The Minds

Five kinds of reviewer

Cluster 98,513 reviewer-forum profiles — one reviewer on one paper — by which standards they habitually invoke, and five archetypes emerge. Each rose has twelve petals — one per standard — sized by how far that reviewer-kind leans on it relative to the field. The names are editorial; the petals are not.

Fig. 6 — k-means (k = 5, seed 7) over per-reviewer reasoning-standard profiles, official reviewers with ≥5 units, 2018–2026; a "reviewer" is one reviewer within one forum. The sparkline is the archetype's share of each year's reviewers. The nine-year story: the Gatekeeper — novelty and polish — is fading (25% → 15%), and the Stress-Tester — robustness, confounds, cost — is inheriting the field (19% → 26%). The Advocate runs 18–23 points less negative than the other four kinds.

Fig. 8 — How to read 8a: each row is one rating; the bar is the verdict mix of its units; the thin brass underbar is the relative number of reviews at that rating; the right column reads n reviews · units per review · mean reviewer confidence (1–5). Ratings are joined at display time only; extraction never saw them. Five findings that are not the tautology (low score = more negative): (1) Scrutiny relocates rather than shrinks — novelty largely disappears from an 8's review (a novelty unit appears in 20% of 2-reviews but only 9% of 8-reviews) and baselines (42% → 29%), but inspects theory, method design, and compute more than a 2 does. (2) A 2's praise is consolation praise — its rare positive units go to clarity and framing ("well written, nice motivation"); an 8's praise goes to empirical scope and theory. (3) There are many ways to fail — within-rating diversity of what reviews discuss falls monotonically from 0 (most heterogeneous) to 10 (most scripted): unhappiness is diverse, excellence has one shape. (4) In split verdicts on the very same paper, both reviewers inspect theory and method at identical rates and merely disagree on the verdict — but novelty is a weapon only the low scorer draws (condemned by 22% of ≤2 reviewers vs 4% of their ≥8 co-reviewers). Quieter still: the reviewer's own confidence follows a U (4.2 at 0 · 3.4 at 6 · 3.9 at 10), and mid-scale criticism is the most actionable (78% of a 2–4's units carry a fix vs 56% at 10). (5) The decision is a mean, not a variance — 93% of multi-review papers carry disagreement (half of them by two full notches), and disagreement peaks for mid-scoring papers; yet holding the exact mean fixed, a unanimous 6-6-6 and a divided 4-6-8 are accepted at the identical rate (92% at mean 6.0). A naive banded version of this analysis suggests "consensus is fate" — that is a mean-composition confound, and the panel averages it away.

Plate IX · The Drift
Corpus: ICLR 2018–2026 · forum-level track · 1,009,592 units

Nine years of shifting scrutiny

The same taxonomy, applied to 1,009,592 units from every ICLR review 2018–2026 (the forum-level Direct track). Share of reviewer attention per year; the four biggest movers are named, the rest recede.

Fig. 9a — What reviewers inspect, 2018 → 2026
Fig. 9b — The standards they invoke, 2018 → 2026
Fig. 9c — The hardening: nine-year vitals

Fig. 9 — How to read 9c: three nine-year vital signs — how often a post-rebuttal judgment weakens or reverses, how much of a review is written after the rebuttal, and how many units a reviewer produces. The hygiene turn: compute cost (+3.2pp), statistical rigor (+3.0pp), baselines and reproducibility rise; clarity (−4.9pp) and theory (−4.1pp) recede. Among standards, the novelty standard and presentation-as-trust each fall 3.5pp while cost-benefit and claim-evidence matching climb — scrutiny has drifted from what is this idea? toward what does the evidence cost, and does it cover the claim? Negativity on this forum-level track holds near 77% all nine years (the 2026 review-level track reads 71.8% — a different memo style, the same story). Assignment similarity is flat across years (0.81), so the drift is not an artifact of taxonomy transfer. And the field is hardening: the share of post-response judgments that weaken or reverse has halved, 7.7% in 2018 to 3.4% in 2026.

Plate X · The Rebuttal
Corpus: ICLR 2018–2026 · forum-level track · Fig. 10c: 2026 only

Author responses barely move the needle

130,650 units sit after the author response (2018–2026). When the reviewer's judgment moves at all, it moves toward reinforcement two and a half times more often than concession — and no kind of response escapes that ratio.

Fig. 10a — Fate of a judgment after the author responds
Fig. 10b — What the response contained, and what happened
Fig. 10c — Who yields? Post-rebuttal movement by position in a split verdict (ICLR 2026)

Fig. 10 — 10a: shares of all post-response units. 10b: among units whose judgment visibly moved, split by a keyword reading of the recorded trigger; red = weakened or reversed, brass = strengthened, grey = clarified only. Even fresh experiments strengthen the original judgment 2.9× more often than they soften it. Softening rates barely differ by topic (3.9–5.4% across all twelve objects). How to read 10c: reviewers on 2026 forums whose ratings split by ≥4 notches, grouped by their own position; oxblood dot = share whose judgment weakened or reversed, brass dot = share that strengthened. The dissenting low scorer is the one who moves — 2.4× likelier to yield than the high co-reviewer — yet even for them reinforcement beats concession, and unanimous panels barely stir at all (9,329 split forums; ratings matched by reviewer signature, 95% coverage).

Plate XI · The Tribunal
Corpus: ICLR 2026 · review-level track · decisions joined at display time

Which criticism travels with rejection

13,704 of the 19,474 reviewed ICLR 2026 papers carry a non-withdrawn decision; the rest are excluded here. For each object of scrutiny: the acceptance rate of papers that drew at least one negative unit there, against those that didn't. Association, not causation — but the asymmetry is stark.

Fig. 11 — Acceptance rate with (●) vs without (○) negative units, per object

Fig. 11 — Novelty criticism shows the largest acceptance gap (−10.6pp, association only); statistical rigor and baseline complaints follow. At the other end, compute-cost and robustness criticism show no acceptance gap at all — papers criticized there are accepted at the same rate as papers that are not. Decisions were never shown to the extraction pipeline; they are joined here for the first time.

Plate XII · The Gauntlet
Corpus: ICLR 2026 · review-level track · decisions joined at display time

Acceptance falls with every new front of criticism

For each ICLR 2026 paper: on how many of the twelve objects did it draw at least one negative unit — and did it survive? Dot size is the number of papers standing at that breadth.

Fig. 12 — Acceptance rate by breadth of criticism

Fig. 12 — Acceptance falls steeply, then almost linearly: 69% at two fronts, 57% at three, then roughly five points per additional front down to 27% at nine or more. Few papers escape with fewer than four: the modal paper is criticized on seven of the twelve objects. Association, not causation — breadth of criticism and paper quality are entangled by design.

Dissent, and the kinder judge

Where do reviewers of the same paper split — and how does the meta-reviewer differ from the panel they oversee?

Fig. 13 — Contested ground: share of forums where reviewers took opposite stances

Fig. 13 — A category counts as contested in a forum when at least two official reviewers touched it and landed on opposite valences. Reviewers split most on problem framing (38.6%) — whether the question matters — and least on robustness (16.3%), where the evidence speaks. Meta-reviewers, meanwhile, run 13.7pp less negative and 10.5pp more positive than the panels they summarize.
